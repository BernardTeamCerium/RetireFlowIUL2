What Is an IUL?

IUL in Plain English

Indexed Universal Life is a type of permanent life insurance. Like any life insurance, it pays a death benefit to your beneficiaries. What makes it different is the cash value component.

Part of each premium goes into the policy's cash value. That cash value can be credited interest based on how a market index, like the S&P 500®, performs. Your money is never directly invested in the market.

How It Works

How an IUL Works

1

Protection first

Your policy provides a death benefit to help take care of the people who depend on you.

2

Growth potential

Cash value can earn interest tied to an index's performance, up to a cap set by the insurer.

3

A floor for down years

If the index goes negative, a 0% floor means no loss is credited to your cash value for that period.

Policy fees, charges, and cost of insurance still apply and can reduce cash value, even in years with 0% credited interest. Caps and participation rates vary by carrier and can change.

Why People Consider an IUL

What People Use It For

Protecting their family

A death benefit that can help replace income, pay off a mortgage, or cover final expenses.

Building a living benefit

Cash value that may be available through policy loans or withdrawals later in life.

Adding to retirement planning

Under current tax law, policy loans may give you tax-advantaged access to cash value, alongside a 401(k) or IRA. It doesn't replace them.

Leaving a legacy

The death benefit generally passes to beneficiaries income-tax-free.

Flexibility

Within policy limits, premiums and death benefits can often be adjusted as life changes.

The Honest Part

It's Not Right for Everyone, and We'll Tell You If It's Not Right for You

An IUL works best when it's designed correctly, funded consistently, and held for the long term. It's a commitment.

Our job is to help you understand all of it, so you can decide with confidence. Sometimes the right answer is term insurance, or something else entirely, and we'll tell you that.

Here's what you should know going in:

  • Caps limit how much interest you can be credited in strong market years.
  • Fees and cost of insurance are higher than term life insurance.
  • Surrender charges usually apply if you cancel in the early years.
  • Loans and withdrawals reduce cash value and death benefit, and could create a tax bill if the policy lapses.
  • Results aren't guaranteed. Illustrations are projections, not promises.

FAQ

Common Questions

Is an IUL an investment?

No. An IUL is a life insurance policy. Its cash value is credited interest based on an index's performance, but your money isn't invested directly in the market.

Can I lose money in an IUL?

The 0% floor means negative index performance won't credit a loss. However, policy charges and fees are deducted from cash value, so cash value can decrease, especially in early years or if the policy is underfunded.

Is the cash value tax-free?

Cash value grows tax-deferred. Under current tax law, policy loans are generally not taxable as long as the policy stays in force and isn't a modified endowment contract (MEC). If the policy lapses with an outstanding loan, taxes may be due. Talk to a tax professional about your situation.

How much does an IUL cost?

It depends on your age, health, coverage amount, and how you want to fund it. Policies can be designed for a range of budgets. We'll walk through real numbers on your call.

How is this different from term life insurance?

Term covers you for a set period, usually 10–30 years, and costs less. An IUL is permanent coverage with a cash value component. Many people benefit from term, some from IUL, and some from both.

Will I get a sales pitch?

No. Your call is an educational session. We'll ask about your goals, explain how things work, and give you an honest take on whether an IUL fits. There's no obligation to buy anything.
